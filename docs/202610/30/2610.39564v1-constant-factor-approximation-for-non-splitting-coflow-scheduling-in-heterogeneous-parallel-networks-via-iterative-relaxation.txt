# Constant-Factor Approximation for Non-Splitting Coflow Scheduling in Heterogeneous Parallel Networks via Iterative Relaxation 

Chi-Yeh Chen Department of Computer Science and Information Engineering, National Cheng Kung University, Taiwan, ROC. chency@csie.ncku.edu.tw. October 30, 2026 

Abstract 

Coflows capture the collective communication requirements of data-parallel applications, and scheduling them well is essential to the performance of modern data centers. We study non-splitting coflow scheduling in a heterogeneous parallel network whose m network cores may be Electronic Packet Switches (EPS), not-all-stop Optical Circuit Switches (OCS), or all-stop OCS, where every flow must be transmitted entirely through a single core. Previous approximation algorithms for this setting have guarantees that grow linearly with the number of cores m

or with the maximum flow degree τ of a port, and therefore become weak as data centers deploy more cores. The source of this dependence is the rounding step: rounding each flow to the core holding its largest fractional value can inflate the load of a port by a factor of m.We replace this step with an iterative relaxation rounding scheme that handles the input-port and output-port capacity constraints simultaneously. For minimizing the makespan, this yields approximation ratios of min {τ, 3}, 2 min {τ, 3}, and 4 min {τ, 3} for pure EPS, pure not-all-stop OCS, and pure all-stop OCS networks, respectively. For minimizing the total weighted coflow completion time, we obtain approximation ratios of 8 min {τ, 4}, 16 min {τ, 4}, and 32 min {τ, 4}

for the same three architectures. In a hybrid network, the guarantee is governed by the weakest architecture present, so the overall ratios are at most 4 min {τ, 3} ≤ 12 and 32 min {τ, 4} ≤ 128, respectively. Unlike all previous guarantees for non-splitting coflow scheduling in heterogeneous parallel networks, these bounds are absolute constants that do not grow with the number of cores m or with the flow degree τ . We also show that the integrality gap of the underlying linear program is at least 2. 

Key words: Scheduling algorithms, approximation algorithms, coflow, iterative relaxation, datacenter network, heterogeneous parallel network. 

# 1 Introduction 

In data-parallel frameworks such as MapReduce [13], Hadoop [27, 3], Dryad [16], and Spark [32], a computation stage produces a large amount of intermediate data that must be shuffled between two groups of machines before the next stage can begin. The coflow abstraction [8] groups the parallel flows of such a communication stage into a single unit. Because the next stage cannot start until all of its input has arrived, the quantity that matters to the application is the completion time of 1arXiv:2610.39564v1 [cs.DS] 29 Oct 2026 the last flow of the coflow [8, 25], and a number of systems have shown that scheduling the network at the granularity of coflows yields substantial gains [11, 9, 34, 1]. The switching fabric of data centers is also becoming increasingly heterogeneous. An Electronic Packet Switch (EPS) multiplexes packets of many flows on a link and allows flows to be preempted. An Optical Circuit Switch (OCS) [35, 28, 21] offers much higher bandwidth at lower energy cost, but each of its ports can maintain only one circuit at any moment, and every change of the circuit configuration costs a reconfiguration delay [35]. Depending on how reconfiguration is carried out, an OCS is either all-stop, in which case all circuits pause during a reconfiguration, or not-all-stop, in which case only the ports being reconfigured are interrupted. A realistic data center therefore contains several network cores of different types and speeds, and a coflow scheduler must decide both which core carries each flow and when the flow is transmitted. We consider a set of coflows, each described by a demand matrix D(k) = ( dijk )Ni,j =1 , that must be transmitted through m heterogeneous network cores, where core q has link rate rq. Flows are not allowed to be split: every flow is carried entirely by one core, although different flows of the same coflow may use different cores. Splitting a flow across fabrics causes packet reordering and other overhead that is undesirable in practice [7]. We study two objectives: the makespan max k{Ck}

The coflow abstraction was introduced by Chowdhury and Stoica [8]. Since then, coflow scheduling has been studied mainly in two families of networks, namely EPS-based and OCS-based networks, and more recently in networks that mix both. 1.1.3 Non-splitting scheduling on heterogeneous parallel networks 

The work most closely related to ours is [7], which gave the first approximation algorithms with provable guarantees for non-splitting coflow scheduling in a hybrid network with an arbitrary num-ber m ≥ 2 of EPS, not-all-stop OCS, and all-stop OCS cores. The algorithms in [7] first solve a linear programming relaxation of the flow-to-core assignment and then round each flow to the core with the largest fractional value. Let τ be the maximum number of flows incident to a single port. For the makespan, the resulting ratios are min {τ, m }, 2 min {τ, m }, and 2 min {2τ − 1, m + τ − 1} in pure EPS, pure not-all-stop OCS, and pure all-stop OCS networks, respectively; for the total weighted coflow completion time, they are 8 min {τ, 2m}, 16 min {τ, 2m}, and 16 min {2τ − 1, 2m + τ − 1} [7]. In a hybrid network, the guarantee is determined by the weakest architecture present. Every one of these ratios grows linearly in m or τ , so they lose their meaning precisely when the number of cores is large. The reason is inherent to the rounding step. The largest of the m

fractional values of a flow is only guaranteed to be at least 1 

> m

, and if many flows of a port are spread evenly over all cores by the linear program and then rounded onto the same core, the load of that port grows by a factor of m. Rounding methods based on bipartite matching [26] control the load of one side, but every assignment variable belongs to both an input-port constraint and an output-port constraint, so matching on the input side alone gives no control on the output side [7]. 

The goal of this paper is to remove the factors m and τ from the approximation ratios of [7]. 

1.2 Our Contributions 

We keep the linear programming relaxations of [7] and replace their rounding step by an iterative relaxation scheme in the spirit of Lau, Ravi, and Singh [19]. Our contributions are as follows. 

• Iterative relaxation rounding for two families of capacity constraints. In the as-signment linear program, each variable appears in exactly one assignment constraint and in exactly two capacity constraints, one for its input port and one for its output port. We show that every extreme point of the residual linear program either contains an integral variable or contains a capacity constraint whose total fractional deficiency is at most 2, and that dropping such a constraint costs at most twice the largest transmission time. Consequently, 3the load of every port on every core after rounding is at most min {τ, 3}T ∗, where T ∗ is the smallest value for which the relaxation is feasible. The previous bound was min {τ, m }T ∗ [7]. 

• Makespan. We obtain approximation ratios of 

– min {τ, 3} in a pure EPS network, 

– 2 min {τ, 3} in a pure not-all-stop OCS network, and 

– 4 min {τ, 3} in a pure all-stop OCS network. For all-stop OCS, we also remove the additive dependence on τ caused by the reconfiguration delays by bounding the number of reconfigurations at a port through the port load itself. 

• Total weighted coflow completion time. Applying the new rounding inside each time interval of the interval-indexed relaxation of [7], we obtain approximation ratios of 

– 8 min {τ, 4} in a pure EPS network, 

– 16 min {τ, 4} in a pure not-all-stop OCS network, and 

– 32 min {τ, 4} in a pure all-stop OCS network. 

• Hybrid networks and limits of the relaxation. In a hybrid network, the overall guar-antee equals that of the weakest architecture present; hence the makespan ratio is at most 12 and the weighted completion time ratio is at most 128, independently of m and τ . We further show that the integrality gap of the makespan relaxation is at least 2, so any analysis based on this relaxation cannot bound the port loads by less than 2 T ∗.

# 3 Approximation Algorithm for Minimizing the Makespan 

Like the algorithms of [7], our algorithm works in two stages. The first stage assigns every flow to a core by rounding a fractional solution of a linear program, and the second stage schedules, independently on each core, the flows that were assigned to it. The second stage is the same as in [7]; the novelty lies in the rounding of the first stage.

# 5 Concluding Remarks 

This paper revisited non-splitting coflow scheduling in heterogeneous parallel networks composed of EPS, not-all-stop OCS, and all-stop OCS cores. The previous algorithms for this problem [7] have approximation ratios that grow linearly with the number of cores m or with the maximum flow degree τ , because rounding each flow to its largest fractional core may multiply the load of a port by m. We replaced this step by an iterative relaxation rounding that controls the input-port and the output-port capacity constraints at the same time, and we proved that the load of every port on every core is at most min {τ, 3}T ∗. For all-stop OCS, we additionally showed that the number of reconfigurations at a port is controlled by its load, which removes the additive τ

term caused by reconfiguration delays. For minimizing the makespan, the resulting approximation ratios are min {τ, 3}, 2 min {τ, 3}, and 4 min {τ, 3} for pure EPS, pure not-all-stop OCS, and pure all-stop OCS networks. For minimizing the total weighted coflow completion time, applying the same rounding within every interval of the interval-indexed relaxation yields approximation ratios of 8 min {τ, 4}, 16 min {τ, 4}, and 32 min {τ, 4}. In a hybrid network, the guarantee is determined by the weakest architecture present, so the overall ratios are at most 12 and 128, respectively. To the best of our knowledge, these are the first approximation ratios for non-splitting coflow scheduling in heterogeneous parallel networks that do not depend on the number of network cores. Several questions remain open. First, Proposition 3.11 shows that the integrality gap of LP( T ) is at least 2, while our rounding achieves a port-load bound of 3 T ∗; closing this gap, for example with a refined rule for dropping constraints or with a strengthened relaxation, would directly improve all of our ratios. Second, the constants for the total weighted completion time come from several sources, including the group threshold 12 , the geometric interval length, and the factor 2 in the capacity of each group, and it would be interesting to reduce them, for example by randomizing the interval boundaries. Third, the schedules on individual OCS cores lose a factor of 2 or 4 relative to the port loads; designing assignment and scheduling procedures that are analyzed jointly may reduce this loss.
